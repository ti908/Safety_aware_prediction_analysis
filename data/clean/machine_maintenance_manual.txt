Machine Maintenance Manual for Predictive Maintenance Engineers
1. Introduction
Modern industrial systems rely heavily on the seamless operation of machinery to sustain productivity, ensure
### safety, and reduce operational costs. However, even with optimized manufacturing environments, machine failures
remain inevitable due to the complex interplay of mechanical stress, environmental conditions, and operational
wear. This manual provides a comprehensive overview of machine maintenance principles using an explainable
artificial intelligence (XAI) framework integrated with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). It draws upon
data-driven insights from the AI4I 2020 Predictive Maintenance Dataset and the foundational research by Matzka
(2020) to guide engineers in understanding, diagnosing, and preventing machine failures in real-world scenarios.
2. Dataset and System Foundation
The predictive maintenance dataset underlying this manual consists of 10,000 data records, each representing a
machine’s operational state. These data points include six measurable parameters, each reflecting the physical
condition of the equipment at a specific point in time:
1. Product ID – A categorical identifier distinguishing product quality levels (L, M, H), affecting component lifespan
and tool wear.

2. Air Temperature [K] – Ambient temperature around 300 K generated via a random-walk process.

4. Rotational Speed [rpm] – Describes machine rotational velocity, directly influencing torque and power output.
5. Torque [Nm] – Represents twisting force during operation, normally distributed around 40 Nm.
6. Tool Wear [min] – Duration for which a tool has been in continuous use, increasing with product quality.
Each record includes a machine failure label: “1” for failure and “0” for normal operation.
3. Understanding Failure Modes
Predictive maintenance revolves around identifying the conditions that lead to failure. The dataset models five
distinct failure modes:
Tool Wear Failure (TWF): Occurs when tool wear exceeds 200–240 minutes. Prevent by monitoring wear-time
counters and scheduling timely replacements.

Tool Wear Failure (TWF): Occurs when tool wear exceeds 200–240 minutes. Prevent by monitoring wear-time
counters and scheduling timely replacements.
Heat Dissipation Failure (HDF): Happens when (Process T – Air T) < 8.6 K and RotSpeed < 1380 rpm, leading to
poor heat transfer. Inspect cooling systems regularly.
Power Failure (PWF): When Torque × RotSpeed falls outside [3500, 9000] W, indicating motor inefficiency.
Compare torque-speed plots to identify weak motors.
Overstrain Failure (OSF): When Torque × Tool Wear exceeds thresholds (11k–13k min·Nm), depending on product
type. Monitor torque and replace tools early.
Random Failure (RNF): A 0.1% random occurrence due to unpredictable factors like surges or transient defects.

The framework employs Explainable AI (XAI) consisting of two methods:
1. Decision Tree Explanations: Simple models identify relationships among torque, speed, and wear. Example rule:
“If torque < 13.2 Nm → failure; if torque > 65 Nm and speed > 1228 rpm → failure.”
2. Normalized Feature Deviation (NFD): Calculates deviations from normal conditions, selecting top deviating
features. Example: “Failure due to high tool wear (221 min) and low rotational speed (1399 rpm).”
These methods combine quantitative thresholds and qualitative insights for clear diagnostics.
5. Maintenance Strategy and Interpretation
Engineers should interpret model predictions as indicators of machine health:
1. Verify Sensor Accuracy.
2. Cross-Validate Conditions with observed parameters.
3. Apply Failure Logic using thresholds in Section 3.
4. Take Preventive Action by scheduling early maintenance.
This ensures predictive alerts translate into actionable maintenance actions.

7. Practical Maintenance Guidelines
- Maintain torque between 13–65 Nm and speed near 1228–1380 rpm.
- Ensure process temperature remains at least 8.6 K above air temperature.
- Replace tools nearing 200 minutes of wear (for L-type).
- Record all anomalies to enhance model retraining.
- Provide a single clear explanation per prediction to ensure trust.
